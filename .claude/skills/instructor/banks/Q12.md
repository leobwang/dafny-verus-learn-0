Predict the stage each run reports (`D:<stage> V:<stage>`, from `cli`, `parse`, `resolve`, `verify`, `compile`, `test`, `run`).

**Target insights**
- The Dafny file verifies and compiles; the compiled test's `expect` fails at run time: stage `test`. A wrong test is not a wrong proof.

### K4 [why] — why arms are compared at equal calls
**viva:** yes · **strand:** S12 · **tags:** `matched-compute` · **backward-edge:** Q06 (pass@k and what it counts)

A model solves a task in one of 8 independent samples. An arm that reports "solved within 8 calls" and an arm that reports "the first sample's success rate" describe the same runs. Why does Lab 1 fix the budget per task in model calls, the same for every arm, and what goes wrong if one arm is reported at pass@1 and another at pass@8?

**Target insights**
- With 1 correct sample in 8, the unbiased pass@1 is 0.125 and pass@8 is 1.0 (`fvh.stats.pass_at_k`): the same runs give either number depending on how many samples the metric lets count.
- An arm compared at a larger budget wins by spending more, not by searching better. Fixing calls per task (B = 8) for every arm makes the comparison about how the calls are used: eight samples, one sample and seven repairs, or eight expansions.
- A call is the unit because it is what the budget pays for; attempts and rounds differ between arms, so the plan writes calls = tasks × attempts × rounds × arms × repeats per arm.

**Misconceptions & counter-moves**
- *"Equal attempts is fair."* → Ask: how many calls is one A2 attempt?
- *"pass@8 is just a better metric."* → Probe: the call; ask: which arm would a pass@8 report favour if the other were reported at pass@1?

**Verifier probe**
```
$ uv run --directory harness python3 -c "from fvh import stats; print(stats.pass_at_k(8, 1, 1), stats.pass_at_k(8, 1, 8), stats.pass_at_k(8, 2, 4))"

### K5 [reconstruct] — the MDE at sixty tasks
**viva:** yes · **strand:** S13 · **tags:** `statistics`, `pre-registration` · **backward-edge:** none

Lab 1 compares two arms on the same 60 tasks, from a baseline rate of 0.3, at α = 0.05 and power 0.8. Reconstruct what `fvh.stats.mde_two_proportions` computes, with and without `paired=True`, and why the paired figure it prints is not smaller.

**Target insights**
- The MDE is the smallest true difference the test detects with the stated power: about 25 points unpaired and about 26 paired at n = 60, p0 = 0.3. Lab 1 cannot detect a 10-point gain; the plan must say so.
- The paired formula (McNemar's) uses the discordance two independent arms would show. Correlated arms disagree on fewer tasks, so the true paired MDE is smaller than the printed value: the printed figure is the conservative one, and the plan states which it used.
- The unit is the task. Three repeats per task do not triple n: repeats of one task are correlated, so treating 180 task-repeats as independent pairs would overstate what the lab can detect.

**Misconceptions & counter-moves**
- *"Pairing always lowers the MDE."* → Probe: both calls; ask: what does the paired formula assume about how often the arms disagree?
- *"Three repeats make n = 180."* → Ask: are two repeats of one task independent observations?

**Verifier probe**
```
$ uv run --directory harness python3 -c "from fvh import stats; print(stats.mde_two_proportions(60, 0.3), stats.mde_two_proportions(60, 0.3, paired=True))"
0.2503940387045733 0.2563446308948465
```

### K6 [connect] — Holm, and what it costs the MDE
**viva:** yes · **strand:** S13 · **tags:** `statistics`, `pre-registration` · **backward-edge:** Q09 (the minimum detectable effect)

Q09's B1 computed an MDE for one comparison. Lab 1 has a family of two (A2 against A1, A3 against A1) under Holm. What α does the first test of the family face, how does that change the paired MDE at n = 60, p0 = 0.3, and which of the three p-value pairs below does Holm reject at 0.05?

**Target insights**
- Holm tests the smallest p-value at α/2 = 0.025 for a family of two, then the next at α. So the plan's MDE is computed at 0.025 for the worst case: about 28 points paired, against about 26 at 0.05.
- `holm([0.03, 0.04])` rejects nothing (0.03 > 0.025, and Holm stops); `holm([0.01, 0.04])` rejects both (0.01 ≤ 0.025, then 0.04 ≤ 0.05); `holm([0.01, 0.02])` rejects both.
- The family is pre-registered: adding a comparison after the data changes every adjusted α, which is why the language interaction is named before any run.

**Misconceptions & counter-moves**
- *"Holm divides every α by the family size."* → Probe: `holm([0.01, 0.04])`; ask: at what level was 0.04 tested?
- *"Correction does not change the MDE."* → Probe: the two MDE calls.

**Verifier probe**
```
$ uv run --directory harness python3 -c "from fvh import stats; print(stats.mde_two_proportions(60, 0.3, paired=True), stats.mde_two_proportions(60, 0.3, alpha=0.025, paired=True))"
0.2563446308948465 0.2845338982401394
```
```
$ uv run --directory harness python3 -c "from fvh import stats; print(stats.holm([0.03, 0.04]), stats.holm([0.01, 0.04]), stats.holm([0.01, 0.02]))"
[False, False] [True, True] [True, True]

### K7 [predict] — a lever dropped from a task
**viva:** yes (verifier-refereed predict) · **strand:** S12 · **tags:** `matched-compute`, `pre-registration` · **backward-edge:** none

A Dafny task form ships `lemma {:induction false} SumAtLeast(n: nat) ensures Sum(n) >= n` with an empty body. One delivery deletes the attribute and keeps the empty body; another keeps the attribute and adds the recursive call. Predict three runs: the dropped delivery with plain `scripts/vcheck`, the dropped delivery with the lab gate (`scripts/vcheck --gate --lab-task <task form> <delivery>`), and the proved delivery with the lab gate (`ok` or `fail` each).

**Target insights**
- With the attribute gone, Dafny's automatic induction proves the lemma by itself: the plain run verifies.
- The lab gate compares the delivery's levers with the task form's and refuses one that drops a lever the task ships: `lab-lever-removed`, a finding, so the gate fails though every obligation verified.
- The proved delivery keeps the lever and writes the induction: the gate verifies.
- Without that refusal, a task could be closed by switching automation back on, and the lab would measure the setting, not the search. That is why the local stratum's composition depends on the lever check (`STRATUM.toml`).

**Misconceptions & counter-moves**
- *"Deleting an attribute cannot help a proof."* → Probe: the plain run.
- *"The gate rejects it because it does not verify."* → Probe: the gate run; ask: how many errors, and what is the finding?

### K8 [transfer] — McNemar per repeat
**viva:** yes · **strand:** S13 · **tags:** `statistics` · **backward-edge:** none

In one repeat, arm A2 solves 9 tasks that A1 does not, and A1 solves 3 that A2 does not; in the pooled repeats the counts are 12 and 6. Lab 1's report runs McNemar per repeat as a sensitivity line beside the bootstrap interval. Transfer: what does McNemar use from the paired table, what does it say here, and why is it a sensitivity line rather than the primary test?

**Target insights**
- McNemar uses only the discordant tasks (solved by one arm and not the other): 9 against 3 gives p ≈ 0.146, 12 against 6 gives p ≈ 0.238. Neither is significant at 0.05, let alone at Holm's 0.025.
- The tasks both arms solve, or both fail, carry no information about the difference: a large concordant count does not make a small discordant split significant.
- The primary analysis resamples tasks over all repeats (the bootstrap of the per-task difference); McNemar per repeat checks that one repeat does not tell a different story.

**Misconceptions & counter-moves**
- *"Nine to three is decisive."* → Probe: `stats.mcnemar(9, 3)`.
- *"Pooling repeats adds evidence."* → Ask: are the pooled pairs independent?

**Verifier probe**
```
$ uv run --directory harness python3 -c "from fvh import stats; print(stats.mcnemar(9, 3), stats.mcnemar(12, 6))"
0.14599609375 0.237884521484375
```
