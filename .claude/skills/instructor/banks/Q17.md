### K1 [predict] — a client typed by the trait
**viva:** yes (verifier-refereed predict) · **strand:** S6 · **tags:** `traits-and-interfaces`, `refinement` · **backward-edge:** Q14 (a trait's contract and a generic client)

A trait `Lantern` promises that `Brighten` raises its ghost `Glow` (Verus: `spec fn glow`), and nothing more. The class `Torch` (Verus: the struct `Torch`) restates the method with a stronger postcondition, an exact rise of 2, and its body meets it. Two clients each call `Brighten` once and assert the rise of 2: `UseAsLantern` holds the object as a `Lantern` (Verus: a generic `L: Lantern`), `UseAsTorch` as a `Torch`. **Target insights**
- Both files fail, and in both it is the client typed by the trait: `D:fail V:fail`. The client typed by the class verifies, because it is checked against the class's own, stronger postcondition.
- A strengthened postcondition is allowed (Dafny checks the restatement is at least as strong; Verus adds the impl's `ensures` to the inherited one), but it is invisible to code that sees only the trait. What a client may conclude is fixed by the type it holds.
- For H1: a client of `PQueue` concludes only what the trait states, whatever `ArrayPQ` adds; the given checks that read the array do so through the class (`Peek`, `ArrayPQCheck`), not through the trait.

### K2 [why] — `this` in the frame
**viva:** yes · **strand:** S6 · **tags:** `frames` · **backward-edge:** Q05 (a method's `modifies` frame)

A class `Kiln` keeps its ghost frame `Repr`, and its validity predicate says only that its array of trays is in `Repr`. Its method `Fire` requires `Valid()`, may modify `Repr`, and adds one to a field of the kiln itself. Why does Dafny refuse the assignment, and what one change to `Valid()` fixes it? What does that change have to do with every method of H1's class, and is there a Verus counterpart?

**Target insights**
- `modifies Repr` licenses writes to the objects in `Repr` only. Nothing says the kiln itself is in `Repr`, so `heat := heat + 1` writes an object outside the frame: "assignment might update an object not in the enclosing context's modifies clause".
- Adding `this in Repr` to `Valid()` fixes it: the method's precondition then puts the object in its own frame.
- In H1 every method writes the object's own fields (the count, the ghost model, the frame itself), and the trait's methods say `modifies Repr`, so the invariant must put the object in `Repr`; the same holds for every array a method writes.
- Verus has no `modifies` clause: a method that takes `&mut self` may write `self`'s fields, so there is no counterpart of this error; ownership plays the frame's part.

**Misconceptions & counter-moves**
- *"`reads this, Repr` on the predicate is enough."* → Probe: the first file; ask: the predicate may read `this`, but what does the method's frame allow it to write?
- *"Add `modifies this` to the method."* → ask: may a class's method modify more than the trait's `modifies Repr` allows, and who checks it?

### K3 [predict] — two objects, two frames
**viva:** yes (verifier-refereed predict) · **strand:** S6 · **tags:** `frames`, `false-friends` · **backward-edge:** Q11 (disjoint frames, `Repr !! other.Repr`)

Two jars, `x` and `y`, each valid, each with its own frame. A client may modify `x.Repr`, calls `x.Shake()` (which may modify everything in `x.Repr` and promises only `x`'s own facts), and then asserts that `y` is still valid and still holds what it held. The file is run twice: once with the precondition `x.Repr !! y.Repr`, once without it. In Verus, `two_jars` takes `x: &mut Jar` and `y: &Jar` and asserts the same of `y`, with no frame at all. Predict each of the three runs (`ok` or `fail`). Then: which of H1's checks would need the disjointness?

**Target insights**
- With the disjointness, `ok`: `Shake` writes only objects of `x.Repr`, so nothing `y.Valid()` reads (its `reads this, Repr`) and nothing in `y` can have changed.
- Without it, `fail`, at every conjunct of `y.Valid()` and at the held amount: the two frames may share an object, `y` itself may be in `x.Repr`, and then `x.Shake()` may have changed it.
- Verus: `ok` with no clause: `&mut x` and `&y` cannot alias, so `y` is unchanged by ownership alone. Dafny says the frame; Verus derives it from the borrow.
- In H1: the trait states each method's frame as `Repr` and its growth as fresh objects, so a client holding two queues with disjoint frames keeps the other queue valid; `ArrayPQCheck` uses one queue, so it needs none of this.

**Misconceptions & counter-moves**
- *"`Shake` changes nothing, so `y` is fine either way."* → Probe: the file without the disjointness; ask: what does the client know about `Shake`'s body?
- *"Verus must need a frame too."* → Probe: the Verus file; ask: can `x` and `y` be the same jar there?

**Verifier probe**

### K5 [connect] — giving up the measure
**viva:** yes · **strand:** S7 · **tags:** `termination-measure`, `hoare-logic` · **backward-edge:** Q03 (the trust lint: `decreases *`)

A Dafny method `Drain` counts its argument down by two until it is not positive, and ensures the result is at most 0; it and its loop carry `decreases *`. The Verus `drain` has the same loop and no `decreases`. What do `scripts/vcheck` and `scripts/vaudit` say about the Dafny file, and `scripts/vcheck` about the Verus one? Connect the answer to H3's interpreter: what does a proof about a loop that need not end promise, and what does the fuel in `Exec` play the part of?

**Target insights**
- Dafny verifies `Drain`: `decreases *` turns off the termination check, and the `ensures` is proved for every run that ends (partial correctness). `vaudit` then reports it three times (the auditor's "may not terminate", and the course lint's `decreases-star` on the method and the loop): the course counts a proof that gave up termination as not done (Q03's trust lint).
- Verus refuses the loop at resolve: an exec loop must have a `decreases` clause unless an attribute exempts it.
- A partial-correctness statement says nothing about a run that never ends. H3's `Exec` makes this explicit with fuel: a run that would need more fuel is `Stuck`, and the generator's soundness speaks only of the runs that end (`Done`), so it needs no measure for the loops it runs.

**Misconceptions & counter-moves**
- *"Verified means it terminates."* → Probe: the Dafny file; ask: which check did `decreases *` remove, and which tool reports it?
- *"Fuel makes the semantics total, so the soundness is total correctness."* → ask: what does the statement conclude about a `Stuck` run?

### K7 [reconstruct] — why the reference comes first
**viva:** yes · **strand:** S8 · **tags:** `frozen-statements`, `fidelity-audit` · **backward-edge:** none

**Target insights**
