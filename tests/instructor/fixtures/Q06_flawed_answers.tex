\section*{Problem B1 (hand-calculation)}\label{ans:B1}
\begin{answer}
\textbf{(a)} \\
$\hat p = 17/20 = 0.85$. \\
$z^2/n = 3.8416/20 = 0.192$ \quad denominator $1 + z^2/n = 1.192$. \\
centre $= (\hat p + z^2/(2n))/(1 + z^2/n) = (0.85 + 0.096)/1.192 = 0.794$. \\
under the root: $\hat p(1 - \hat p)/n + z^2/(4n^2) = 0.1275/20 + 3.8416/1600 =
0.006375 + 0.002401 = 0.00878$, root $0.0937$. \\
half-width $= z \times 0.0937 = 1.96 \times 0.0937 = 0.184$. \\
interval: $(0.794 - 0.184,\ 0.794 + 0.184) = (0.610,\ 0.978)$ \quad width: $0.368$

\textbf{(b)} Wald: $0.85 \pm 1.96\sqrt{0.006375} = 0.85 \pm 0.156 = (0.694,\ 1.006)$. \\
The property it breaks: its upper end is above 1, and a success rate cannot
exceed 1. \\
How its shape differs from Wilson's: it is symmetric about $\hat p = 0.85$,
while the Wilson interval is centred at $0.794$, pulled toward $1/2$.

\textbf{(c)} Factor: about 4, so 80 goals \quad because the half-width falls
like $1/\sqrt{n}$: four times the goals halves it.
\end{answer}

\section*{Problem B2 (hand-calculation)}\label{ans:B2}
\begin{answer}
\textbf{(a)} $\binom{5}{2} = 10$ for every task. \\
t1 ($c = 0$): $1 - \binom{5}{2}/\binom{5}{2} = 1 - 10/10 = 0$. \\
t2 ($c = 2$): $1 - \binom{3}{2}/\binom{5}{2} = 1 - 1/10 = 0.9$. \\
t3 ($c = 4$): $n - c = 1 < k = 2$, so pass@2 $= 1$.

\textbf{(b)} pass@2 $= (0 + 0.9 + 1)/3 = 0.633$ \quad pass@1 $= (0/5 + 2/5 + 4/5)/3 = 0.4$

\textbf{(c)} Plug-in for t2: $1 - (1 - 2/5)^2 = 1 - 0.36 = 0.64$ \quad above
or below: below the unbiased value. \\
What the plug-in assumes: that the two attempts are independent draws with
replacement at a known rate $c/n$; the definition counts pairs of distinct
samples among the five observed.
\end{answer}

\section*{Problem B3 (concept)}\label{ans:B3}
\begin{answer}
\textbf{(a)} Pass rate: $1/4 = 0.25$ (only t2 succeeds at its first attempt)
\quad successes among all records: $3/9 = 0.333$ \quad tasks solved at some
attempt: $3/4 = 0.75$.

\textbf{(b)} The rate is a claim about tasks: how often one fresh attempt at a
task like these succeeds, so each task is one observation. The records of one
task are not independent: they share the task's difficulty, and a second
record exists only because the first failed, so a hard task leaves more records
than an easy one. The share of records therefore mixes the prover with the
retry cap (raise the cap and it moves while the prover stays the same), and the
share of solved tasks measures success within three tries, a different and
larger quantity. Counting every record would give $n = 9$ for what is evidence
about 4 tasks, and an interval narrower than the evidence supports.

\textbf{(c)} The attempt budget (three), whether each retry saw the earlier
failures or was an independent sample, how many tasks, and what counts as a
success. Without them the figure compares with neither a first-attempt rate nor
a pass@$k$ from independent samples.
\end{answer}

\section*{Problem B4 (hand-calculation)}\label{ans:B4}
\begin{answer}
\textbf{(a)} Pass rates: A $15/30 = 0.5$, B $24/30 = 0.8$ \\
The informative tasks: only the discordant ones, 11 of them; the 14 both solve
and the 5 both miss say nothing about which is better. \quad $b = 1$, $c = 10$

\textbf{(b)} $X \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(11, 1/2)$, $\min(b, c) = 1$: \\
$P(X \leq 1) = \bigl(\binom{11}{0} + \binom{11}{1}\bigr)/2^{11} = (1 + 11)/2048 = 12/2048$,
so $p = 12/2048 = 0.00586$. \\
Decision at $\alpha = 0.05$: $p < 0.05$, so reject that the two provers are
equally good; B solves more.

\textbf{(c)} In (a): the rates become $141/156 = 0.904$ and $150/156 = 0.962$,
much closer. \quad In (b): nothing changes; $b$ and $c$ are still 1 and 10,
and the concordant tasks never enter the test.
\end{answer}

\section*{Problem B5 (paper card)}\label{ans:B5}
\begin{answer}
\textbf{Source:} cheatsheet entry 02.4, lines 672--699 (the pinned range).

\textbf{Task shape:} each task is one function of a real verified Rust system,
given as a Verus file that keeps types, spec functions and the signatures it
depends on, with the proof bodies removed and the spec unchanged; the agent
must supply the proofs. 849 tasks from 8 systems, long files (about 947 lines,
496 of them spec), few loops.

\textbf{Oracle:} Verus must accept the file with the spec untouched; a cheat
checker (\texttt{verus-checker}) catches cheating, and the hands-off agent can
run both itself.

\textbf{What is trusted:} Verus and its solver, \texttt{vstd}, the extraction
by \texttt{verus log-all}, the cheat checker, and the given specs and lemma
signatures, which can be wrong (the Atmosphere run found 5 spec bugs).

\textbf{Cheat surface:} changing the spec or skipping a proof; the specs are
fixed, the hands-off prompt warns against cheating, and the checker cuts
cheating from up to 14\,\% to under 1.5\,\%, so a residue stays.

\textbf{Metric:} the share of the 849 tasks proved per model and setting:
Sonnet 4.5 proves 81\,\% hands-on and 67\,\% hands-off; o4-mini 17 vs 41\,\%,
GPT-5 50 vs 55\,\%, Sonnet 4 64 vs 58\,\% (hands-off vs hands-on); AutoVerus
20\,\%. Also 83\,\% on Atmosphere and 16/17 from partial human proofs.

\textbf{Budget:} hands-on stops when verified, after 20 minutes or after 20
steps; hands-off Sonnet 4.5 took 7.2 minutes and USD 5.61 per task. The two
settings differ in harness and budget, so the comparison is confounded.

\textbf{Variance:} none reported; the results appear to be single runs, with no
repeats, seeds or intervals.

\textbf{Artifact:} VeruSAGE-Bench, 849 Verus proof tasks from 8 systems, and the
hands-on VeruSAGE agent; only Atmosphere is safely held out.
\end{answer}
