\section*{Problem B1}\label{ans:B1}
\begin{answer}
\textbf{Task shape.} Statute rules in English (150 Texas Transportation Code
rules, 77 Texas Parks and Wildlife Code rules) are formalized into many-sorted
first-order logic over a fixed schema of about 200 predicates:
$y^{orig}=T_1(x)$, back-translated $x'=T_2(y^{orig})$, re-formalized
$y^{rt}=T_3(x')$. An LLM diagnoser $D(x,y^{orig},x',y^{rt})\to(i,e)$ names the
first failed stage $i$ (checked in order) and an explanation $e$ about that
stage only; repair $R_i$ reruns the later stages, at most $K=3$ times.
Baselines: no repair, a random stage, regenerate all.

\textbf{Oracle.} Z3 checks that the two formalizations are equivalent: the
check passes when $\neg(y^{orig}=y^{rt})$ is unsat. No ground truth is used.
Bidirectional BART-MNLI on $(x,x')$ (drift = Unrelated or Contradiction) is a
post hoc proxy only, never in the loop.

\textbf{Trust.} Z3 and the fixed schema; the LLMs that play $T_1$, $T_2$,
$T_3$, the diagnoser and the repairer; a noisy NLI proxy. A passing check
certifies that two formalizations agree, not that either states the rule: the
excerpt names no human faithfulness label.

\textbf{Cheat surface.} The check misses errors that $T_1$ and $T_3$ make in
the same way (stated in the card); a loop that optimizes the check can pass a
consistent but unfaithful pair (about 16\% of passing rules still drift). The
diagnoser is a judge with its own bias: GPT's blames $T_1$ 83--97\% of the
time, 9--16\% without the sequential prompt.

\textbf{Metric.} The share of rules whose two formalizations are equivalent at
the end, per cell: Traffic/Claude 44.7 (none), 64.7 (regenerate), 85.3 (full);
Traffic/GPT 61.3, 84.7, 82.7, and 90.7 with a Claude diagnoser;
Wildlife/Claude 62.3, 81.8, 85.7; Wildlife/GPT 66.2, 84.4, 77.9, and 94.7 with
a Claude diagnoser. This is a pass rate of the check, not a faithfulness rate.
Under full repair, failing rules drift 1.45x--2.53x more than passing ones
(1.74x pooled over the 14 configurations).

\textbf{Budget.} At most $K=3$ repair iterations; a Claude diagnoser cuts
full repair's calls per repair from 14.56 to 7.86 and from 26.44 to 6.05. The
excerpt gives no token, dollar or wall-clock budget and does not say that the
baselines ran at equal cost.

\textbf{Variance.} One run each at temperature 0.3; no interval or repeat is
reported. Small SAT pools, Texas statutes only, rules formalized in isolation;
the 26 rules no method repaired involve schema gaps, exceptions, compound
conditions and conditional thresholds.

\textbf{Artifact.} arXiv 2604.25031 and the local full presentation (lit-rev
03, paper 10). The excerpt names no code or data release; this answer uses only
the hash-bound excerpt.

\textbf{Roundtrip in R15.} The specification under test plays $y^{orig}$:
there is no $T_1$, because Lab $\beta$ judges a given specification, not a
translation of prose. \texttt{roundtrip} makes $T_2$ (spec $\to$ English, the
code redacted) and $T_3$ (English $\to$ spec, from the signature and the
description only) with the pinned Qwen model, and its equivalence check is two
implication lemmas (original $\Rightarrow$ returned and back) decided by the
verifier, with the verdicts equivalent, weaker, stronger, incomparable,
unknown and unparsed. It has no diagnoser and no repair. Its blind class is
the card's: an error that both ends make the same way. Here that is any defect
that a faithful description carries: if $T_2$ describes C4 accurately
(``the leftmost position at which a matching suffix starts'') or C1 (``the end of
the shortest non-empty matching prefix''), $T_3$ rebuilds the same statement,
both implications verify, and the verdict is equivalent. The same holds when
both stages share the model's prior, for instance reading ``shortest match''
as non-empty in both directions: then C1 passes even through a vaguer
description. C3's unsatisfiable requires, described as written, also returns
equivalent. So \texttt{roundtrip} measures whether the description carries the
whole statement, not whether the statement says what the card means.

\texttt{spectest} adds a ground that no model touches: the card's reference
input/output pairs and outputs mutated away from them, each decided by the
verifier. Soundness rejects a statement that refuses a correct pair: C6 and C4
on the pair \texttt{(Chr('a'), ['a','b'])} $\to$ (found, 1), C1 on
\texttt{(Eps, [])} $\to$ (found, 0). Completeness kills mutants that a weak
statement lets through: the mutant (not found) of a pair where a prefix
matches survives C5. Its failure modes are different from \texttt{roundtrip}'s
(finitely many pairs, equivalent mutants), and that difference is what makes
agreement informative: two oracles that fail on different inputs agree often
only when the statements are clear-cut. They can still fail together. A
vacuous requires (Lab $\beta$'s ``vacuous on a case'' variant) passes
\texttt{spectest} at a pair whose input the requires excludes, because the
pair check takes the specification's requires as hypotheses: the check holds
vacuously and every mutant reads as killed; and \texttt{roundtrip} reproduces
it. Agreement is therefore not accuracy: only the hidden verdicts, compared
after the batch, measure each oracle's errors.
\end{answer}
\section*{Problem B2}\label{ans:B2}
\begin{answer}
\textbf{Task shape.} An audit of 5 Lean benchmarks and 13 released variants
(10,318 problems, not deduplicated) for dataset defects and evaluation
failures: static checkers written as Lean 4 metaprograms, LLM prompts, and 20
corrected statements re-evaluated with provers.

\textbf{Oracle.} The Lean kernel certifies that a proof establishes the formal
statement only; it says nothing about fidelity or the harness. Fidelity
findings come from checkers (certified findings: the Axiom and counterexample
columns), from semantic guard proving (pose a hazard's guard and try
\texttt{assumption}, \texttt{simp} and \texttt{omega}; a finding only if all
three fail) and from LLM classification tested on a 55-item false-positive set
and a 92-problem semantic set (552 classifications).

\textbf{Trust.} The kernel; the checkers' metaprograms; an LLM classifier of
low precision; small labelled sets; a final human audit, which the card
recommends (checker + LLM + human).

\textbf{Cheat surface.} Evaluation loopholes (the \texttt{apply?} bug in Lean
before 4.20.0, \texttt{native\_decide}, injected axioms, permissive tactics);
misformalized statements that are easier than the problem; vacuity
($\forall N'<N,\ \neg\mathrm{Periodic}(W,N')$ is contradictory because
\texttt{Periodic W 0} holds by \texttt{rfl}); totalization hazards
($a\mathbin{\dot-}b$, $a/0=0$, \texttt{sqrt} of a negative). RL provers exploit
whatever pays reward.

\textbf{Metric.} Static findings 4,833, of which 398 certified (Axiom column
199, counterexample column 79); the LLM false-positive filter on ProverBench
427 $\to$ 277 ($-35\%$) with every known true positive kept; semantic audit
with Sonnet 4.5 + Thinking: P 0.30, R 0.82, F1 0.42; F1 0.51 on specification
errors against 0.18 on formalization errors; corrected unprovable items 0/20
$\to$ 3/20 (DeepSeek-Prover-V2-7B) and 2/20 (Kimina-Prover-8B).

\textbf{Budget.} The excerpt gives no compute, token or call budget.

\textbf{Variance.} The findings are signals, not prevalence estimates
(the card says so); labelled sets of 55, 92 and 20; the inflation effect on
ProofNet\# is not quantified; minor reporting inconsistencies (398 against 399
proven; recall 0.82 against 0.87); no repeated runs.

\textbf{Artifact.} ICML 2026, arXiv 2606.29493; code released; the checkers are
Lean-only. The card recommends \texttt{proof\_wanted}, \texttt{autoImplicit
false}, no axioms, pinned versions and strict harnesses.

\textbf{(a) The six candidates.} C2 has no defect. C5 is a specification
error: it omits the \texttt{!found} clause, a weaker statement fixed by adding.
C4 and C6 are formalization errors: C4 misrepresents ``prefix'' as
``suffix'' (\texttt{s[e..]} for \texttt{s[..e]}: a correct body fails it, a
suffix search meets it); C6 adds a false obligation (\texttt{e == |s|}) and is
unprovable on inputs whose shortest match is a proper prefix. C1 is a
formalization error of the domain/definition kind: it changes which ends
count by excluding the empty prefix (\texttt{0 < e}), and is unprovable where
the empty prefix matches. C3 is vacuity: its requires fails at the degenerate
instance $k=|s|$ (the whole string as its own prefix), as 05.2's example fails
at $N'=0$. The card's F1 of 0.18 on formalization errors is a warning that C1,
C4 and C6 are the ones an LLM reviewer misses; this module classifies by
witness and instrument instead.
The six statements use no subtraction, division or square root, so the
totalization hazards do not arise; the nearest Verus analogue is
\texttt{subrange}, whose bounds are only recommended, so a wrong quantifier
bound yields an unspecified sequence where Dafny reports a well-formedness
error. The card's Lean-encoding class becomes twin agreement: the Dafny and
Verus forms of each candidate must say the same thing (they do here).

\textbf{(b) Lab $\beta$'s card audit.} For each of the 24 cards: the faithful
specification against the card's prose (a specification or formalization
error there makes the hidden verdict wrong for all four variants; a prose that
is not the one the reference was written from is the card's ``wrong NL
source''); each defective variant against its label, with a witness (a
``weak'' variant equivalent to the faithful one is mislabelled); every
requires for satisfiability, with a witness input, and the vacuous variant's
excluded case shown to be a legitimate input; the reference pairs against the
faithful specification (soundness must hold on it) and their coverage of the
distinguishing inputs (an empty slice, a match away from the start); the
mutation operators for equivalent mutants; the delivery harness for loopholes
(the statement freeze, the hole count and the audit, the lever check, hidden
truth out of every loop, unknown never counted as holding); maintenance
(pinned toolchains, card hashes). As in 05.2, what the audit finds on 24 cards
is a signal, not a prevalence estimate.

\textbf{(c) Loopholes.} Injected axioms have an analogue (Dafny
\texttt{\{:axiom\}}, a bodyless lemma, \texttt{assume}; Verus
\texttt{admit()}, \texttt{assume}, \texttt{external\_body}, \texttt{uninterp}):
the gate's hole count and \texttt{vaudit} report them. Permissive tactics have
a partial analogue in levers and options (\texttt{\{:verify false\}}, resource
or solver options, attributes): \texttt{vaudit} and the lab gate's lever check
report them. The \texttt{apply?} bug has none: it is a defect of one Lean
version's tactic, and the course has no tactic that inserts proof terms; a
verifier soundness bug remains in the trusted base (R13), not a known
loophole of the wrapper. \texttt{native\_decide} has none: it bypasses a small
kernel by trusting compiled evaluation, and Dafny and Verus have no kernel to
bypass; their whole verifier and solver are already trusted. The wrapper has
loophole classes the card does not list: a changed statement
(\texttt{specfreeze}), a verdict read from an exit code or a coarse stage, and
hidden truth leaking into a loop.
\end{answer}
\section*{Problem B3}\label{ans:B3}
\begin{answer}
Instruments: (i) a faithful body against the candidate; (ii) the trivial body
\texttt{found, e := false, 0} (Verus \texttt{None}); (iii) a caller. The Dafny
and Verus forms agree for every candidate: \texttt{Some(e)} is
\texttt{found} with that \texttt{e}, \texttt{None} is \texttt{!found}, and
\texttt{s@.subrange(0, k)} and \texttt{s@.subrange(k, s.len() as int)} are
\texttt{s[..k]} and \texttt{s[k..]}. C2 is the agent file's statement.

\textbf{C1 --- empty-string error (\texttt{0 < e}).} Witness \texttt{r = Eps},
\texttt{s = []}: the faithful answer is found, \texttt{e = 0}. C1 forbids it
(\texttt{0 < e <= 0} is impossible) and its \texttt{!found} clause is false too
(\texttt{Matches(Eps, [])}), so no output meets C1 there; likewise at
\texttt{(Star(Chr('a')), ['a'])}, where the empty prefix is the shortest
match. Instrument (i): a faithful body fails; the trivial body fails as well.

\textbf{C2 --- faithful.} Found implies a matching prefix ending at
\texttt{e} and no shorter matching prefix, empty included; not found implies
no prefix matches, empty and whole included; no requires. At every
\texttt{(r, s)} it forces exactly the faithful answer, so no witness exists. A
faithful body verifies, the trivial body fails at \texttt{(Eps, [])}, and every
caller can call it and conclude both ways.

\textbf{C3 --- vacuous.} Its requires fails for every \texttt{s} at
\texttt{k = |s|}, where \texttt{s[..|s|] == s} (for \texttt{s = []}, at
\texttt{k = 0}). Witness: any input, e.g. \texttt{(Chr('a'), ['a'])}: the
faithful answer is found, \texttt{e = 1}, but no caller may call C3, so it
promises nothing and every body, the trivial one included, meets it.
Instrument (iii): a call at any concrete input fails its precondition, and the
refutation at a concrete \texttt{s} (\texttt{['a'][..1] == ['a']}) verifies.
Whether the verifier also confirms a trivial body depends on its finding the
contradiction; the caller is the reliable instrument. ``Verifies with every
body'' is the symptom, not a sign of faithfulness.

\textbf{C4 --- anchoring error (suffixes).} Witness \texttt{r = Chr('a')},
\texttt{s = ['a','b']}: the faithful answer is found, \texttt{e = 1}. No
suffix (\texttt{['a','b']}, \texttt{['b']}, \texttt{[]}) matches, so C4 forces
not found. Instrument (i): a faithful body returns found there and fails C4. A
search for the leftmost matching suffix verifies against C4: the statement
accepts the wrong program.

\textbf{C5 --- weak (no \texttt{!found} clause).} Witness \texttt{r = Eps},
\texttt{s = []}: the faithful answer is found, \texttt{e = 0}; C5 allows not
found. Instrument (ii): the trivial body verifies. A faithful body verifies
too, so (i) shows nothing; a caller at \texttt{(Chr('a'), ['a'])} cannot
conclude found. This is the loosening the hostile brief invites.

\textbf{C6 --- over-strong (\texttt{e == |s|}).} Witness \texttt{r = Chr('a')},
\texttt{s = ['a','b']}: found would need \texttt{e = 2} and
\texttt{Matches(Chr('a'), ['a','b'])}, which is false; not found would need no
matching prefix, but \texttt{s[..1]} matches. No output meets C6 there, so no
body can. Instrument (i): a faithful body fails the last ensures; the trivial
body fails too (at \texttt{(Eps, [])}). Inputs with no match, or whose shortest
matching prefix is the whole string, hide the defect.

\textbf{What a verified body says.} That this body meets this statement, and
nothing about whether the statement is the intended one: C3 and C5 accept a
body that never searches, C4 accepts a wrong search and rejects the right one,
C1 and C6 reject every body on some inputs. Statement review needs a witness
per verdict and the three instruments together; a contractor's green gate on
a loosened statement is exactly what it catches.
\end{answer}
\section*{Problem B4}\label{ans:B4}
\begin{answer}
\textbf{My reading, before any pass.} The flawed Lab $\beta$ pre-registration
that B4 gives breaks every method dimension:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{hypothesis-precision.} ``$\kappa$ is the share of specifications
both oracles judge correctly, so $\kappa = 0.6$ means each oracle is right about
60\% of the time.'' $\kappa$ is chance-corrected agreement between two raters,
not either rater's accuracy; two oracles that pass the same vacuous variants
raise it. ``Validated if $\kappa\ge0.6$'' cannot be refuted as stated. ``We
look at $\kappa$ after every 48 units and stop once its interval excludes
0.4'' is an interim look on the estimand.
\item \textbf{budget-parity.} It counts ``24 $\times$ 4 $\times$ 2 = 192
roundtrip calls'' while computing $\kappa$ over ``all 192 units''. Recomputed:
192 units $\times$ 2 calls = 384 calls (192 runs); with 768 delivery calls
(192 runs), 1,152 calls and 384 runs against the registry's 960 and 288.
``Sentinel calls \ldots\ are not counted'': the registry's 90 Claude calls are
80 + 10 sentinels. ``No fallback is needed'' rests on the assumed 7.3 s, with
no measured cost and no truncation line.
\item \textbf{variance.} ``The 192 units are independent'' and an MDE from
\texttt{mde\_two\_proportions(192, 0.5)}: the eight units of a card are one
cluster, there is no test and hence no MDE; the width of $\kappa$'s
card-cluster interval is what must be simulated. ``The Claude arm uses seeds
1--20'': Claude has repeated runs.
\item \textbf{contamination.} ``All 24 cards are new, so contamination is
impossible and no perturbation probe or card audit is needed.'' Authorship
date is one control; ten cards are written by the person who builds the
oracles; without the audit, 05.2's defects stay in the hidden verdicts.
\item \textbf{cheat-audit.} ``After each round the loop also tells the model
whether the hidden verdict for its specification is faithful'' puts hidden
truth inside the delivery loop, against the validity rule. ``An unparsed or
unknown roundtrip reply counts as equivalent'' reads a failure as agreement.
\item \textbf{disclosure.} ``Claude is not a subject in Lab $\beta$ because the
oracles use Qwen'': the Claude delivery arm is a subject, and Claude grades
the block. ``plan-sha256: to be filled in after the batch'': no binding.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Passes.} This answer records no live pass. A complete answer pastes
each of the two fresh passes' first line, its six scores in the rubric's
order and its findings at 2 or lower verbatim; compares them per dimension;
marks each flaw above caught by pass 1, pass 2 or neither, with the pass's
quote; disputes any score of 2 or lower that exhibits nothing; and draws a
bounded licence: \texttt{/grade-paper} is an advisory unlock whose misses I
have measured on one exhibit, two passes of one model are not independent
raters, their agreement is not accuracy, and the gradebook row of the
exhibit-grading mode never unlocks a batch.
\end{answer}

\end{answer}

\section*{Problem B6 (pre-registration)}\label{ans:B6}
\begin{answer}
\textbf{Claim.} On these 24 cards with the pinned Qwen model, the two oracles'
binary faithfulness verdicts agree beyond chance by $\kappa$, reported as
$\hat\kappa$ with its card-cluster interval; no test. I expect low to moderate
agreement: the oracles fail on different inputs (\texttt{roundtrip} on errors a
description carries, \texttt{spectest} on inputs its pairs miss), except on
vacuous variants, where both pass. An interval wholly above 0.6 would refute
``low to moderate''; one that includes 0 would mean the oracles' verdicts
share nothing beyond their base rates. $\kappa$ is agreement, not accuracy:
each oracle's accuracy is read only from the descriptive confusion matrices
against the hidden verdicts.

\textbf{Metric and estimator.} Unit: one specification (card $\times$ variant
$\times$ language), 192 units in 24 clusters. \texttt{spectest} says faithful
when the specification is sound on every reference pair and every decided
mutant is killed, with at least one decided mutant; any unknown pair, or no
decided mutant, makes the unit unknown. \texttt{roundtrip} says faithful when
its verdict is equivalent; unknown and unparsed are unknown. $\hat\kappa$ is
Cohen's $\kappa$ pooled over units decided by both oracles; its 95\% interval
resamples whole cards (10,000 resamples, \texttt{random.Random(20261027)}),
recomputing pooled $\kappa$ each time. (\texttt{fvh.stats.cluster\_bootstrap\_ci}
resamples a mean of cluster values, so it is not $\kappa$'s interval; the
resampling is written out.) Sensitivity: unknowns set to disagree and to
agree. Descriptive, after the batch, in the grader container only: each
oracle's 2$\times$2 confusion matrix against the hidden verdicts, with
sensitivity and specificity and Wilson intervals (\texttt{fvh.stats.wilson\_interval}),
by variant and language; the verified-but-unfaithful rate of deliveries per
language (verified = re-judged by the gate in the verification container;
faithful = the delivered specification equivalent both ways to the hidden
reference statement), and its Dafny $-$ Verus difference paired by card with a
card-cluster interval, no test; the oracles' unknown rates.

\textbf{Arms, tasks and the Claude subset.} 24 vericoding cards: the
learner's 10 and the author's 14, each with four variants in both languages.
Qwen \texttt{roundtrip} arm (the primary); Qwen delivery arm (verifier feedback
only, the harness's action schema); Claude delivery arm on 20 cards, 10 per
language, drawn by a pre-registered seed stratified by language, descriptive.
Prompt hashes of the description, formalization and delivery prompts and the
sha256 of \texttt{spectest.py} and \texttt{roundtrip.py} at the run are
recorded.

\textbf{Budget arithmetic.} The primary set needs \texttt{roundtrip} on 24
$\times$ 4 $\times$ 2 = 192 units: 192 runs $\times$ 2 calls = 384 calls.
PLAN's tabled row (96 runs, 192 calls) covers one language. With PLAN's eight
delivery repeats (24 $\times$ 1 $\times$ 4 $\times$ 1 $\times$ 8 = 768 calls,
192 runs) the Qwen total would be 1,152 calls and 384 runs, over the
registry's 960 and 288. Registered instead: deliveries 24 cards $\times$ 1
attempt $\times$ 4 rounds $\times$ 1 arm $\times$ 4 repeats (2 per language) =
384 calls, 96 runs. Qwen: 384 + 384 = 768 calls $\le$ 960; 192 + 96 = 288 runs
$\le$ 288. Claude: 20 $\times$ 1 $\times$ 4 $\times$ 1 $\times$ 1 = 80 calls,
20 runs, plus at most 10 sentinels: $\le$ 90 calls $\le$ 90. A run that stops at
its first verified round spends fewer calls; these are ceilings.

\textbf{Generation settings.} Qwen through the Ollama gate: the pinned model
and digest, thinking off, \texttt{num\_predict} 768, \texttt{num\_ctx} 16384,
temperature 0.7, identical in both Qwen arms. Seeds, one per run, pairwise
disjoint: \texttt{roundtrip} 15001--15192, deliveries 15501--15596. Claude:
the harness's pinned argv; its runs are repeated runs, never seeds.

\textbf{Cost per arm.} Logged per call: tokens in and out, GPU-seconds, wall
clock. Estimate [A] at 7.3 s per call: \texttt{roundtrip} 384 $\times$ 7.3 =
2,803 GPU-s (0.78 GPU-h); deliveries the same; Claude no GPU.

\textbf{GPU-hours against the ceiling.} 768 $\times$ 7.3 s = 5,606 s $\approx$
1.56 GPU-h $\le$ 3. The plan tolerates at most 10,800 / 768 $\approx$ 14.1
GPU-s per call. Lab $\alpha$'s apparatus measured 72 GPU-s per call
(\texttt{num\_predict} 32,768, about 11.5k output tokens; $\Delta$292): at that
cost 768 calls are 15.4 GPU-h, so the settings above are part of the budget,
and the cost per call is measured under them (DV4 step 3d's probe, re-run at
these settings) before the batch.

\textbf{Truncation rate.} The share of calls that end at \texttt{num\_predict},
per arm. A truncated \texttt{roundtrip} reply is unparsed (unknown); a
truncated delivery is a failed round. Sensitivity: $\hat\kappa$ and the
delivery rates recomputed with truncated units excluded.

\textbf{Precision.} No test, so no MDE. Simulation (standard library, seed
