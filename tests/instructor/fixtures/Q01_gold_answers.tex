\section*{Problem B1 (paper card)}\label{ans:B1}
\begin{answer}
\textbf{Source:} cheatsheet entry 02.3, lines 642--668 (the pinned range).

\textbf{Task shape:} ``Vericoding'': the model is handed a formal spec in
Dafny, Verus or Lean with the code and the proof holed out, and must supply
both so the file passes its checker. Each task stays under 100 lines; the
specs come from verification and coding benchmarks and from NumPy and BigNum
documentation.

\textbf{Oracle:} Two conditions, both required: the completed file contains no
bypass pattern, and the checker accepts it. If either fails, its errors go back
to the model for another turn.

\textbf{Trust:} The spec is assumed to say what the code should do, although
5,735 of the tasks are machine translations or autoformalizations; beyond that,
the three checkers and the bypass scanner.

\textbf{Cheat surface:} Bypasses the scanner does not catch, and specs that are
too weak or wrong after translation: in the hand check of solved tasks, nearly
a tenth of the specs were too weak and some 15\% badly translated, and an empty
output met a spec meant for sort-and-dedupe.

\textbf{Metric:} Share of tasks solved, per language, reported twice: for the
best single model (Dafny 67.5\%, Verus 30.9\%, Lean 17.9\%) and for the union
of all nine models (82.2\%, 44.3\%, 26.8\%).

\textbf{Budget:} At most 5 turns per task for each of the 9 models (10 for
BigNum). My card gives no token, cost or time figures.

\textbf{Variance:} Not reported: no repeated runs and no intervals. To know
whether the language gaps exceed run-to-run noise I would need several runs per
model and task; the task pools also differ between languages.

\textbf{Artifact:} A benchmark of 12,504 specs (10,868 after quality checks) in
all three languages, scored with a weighted issue count. My card does not say
where it lives or under which licence.
\end{answer}
