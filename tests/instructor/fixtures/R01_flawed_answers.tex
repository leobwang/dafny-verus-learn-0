\section*{Problem B1}\label{ans:B1}
\begin{answer}
\textbf{Task shape:} A position paper proposing a compiler that checks annotated software through mathematical reasoning, with legacy and open-source software as its test bed; it reports no programme-synthesis experiment.

\textbf{Oracle:} The proposal requires the compiler itself to be formally verified before any proof can count as correct.

\textbf{Trust:} The proposal explicitly permits an unverified verifier. Its language semantics, annotations and proof-checking chain remain part of the trusted account; the card identifies that account as under-specified.

\textbf{Cheat surface:} Counting verified lines can reward weak annotations, so a larger figure need not mean more faithful specifications or more useful guarantees.

\textbf{Metric:} The paper measures an 80 percent success rate on the programmes compiled in its experiments.

\textbf{Budget:} The ambition is a programme on the scale of about one thousand person-years and more than ten years. No per-task model calls, token allowance or solver cap is specified in the pinned range.

\textbf{Variance:} Three repeated compiler trials establish a 95 percent confidence interval for that measured success rate.

\textbf{Artifact:} The range identifies a programme and a proposed legacy-code test bed, not a released compiler implementation or dataset with a reusable location and licence.
\end{answer}
