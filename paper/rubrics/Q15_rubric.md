## Verifier anchors

Every probe runs in `instructor/scratch/` or from the course root as written, one job at a time; the output is pasted verbatim. Arithmetic is recomputed by a command, never by eye. A verified probe confirms a claim; an error verdict alone refutes nothing.

- **Probe H** (B1, the pre-registration's hash). `sed -n '/^% PREREG-BEGIN/,/^% PREREG-END/p' paper/Q15_answers.tex | sha256sum`. Missing markers, or markers that appear more than once: B1 is not graded.
- **Probe B** (B1, the budget arithmetic). For each line of the plan's budget, the product it states, recomputed: `python3 -c 'print(<tasks> * <attempts> * <rounds> * <arms> * <repeats>)'` with the plan's own factors, the runs the same way (tasks × arms × repeats), and the sums against the registry's `budget` for Q15 (`python3 scripts/check_course_map.py --resolve Q15` prints it). A stated total that the product does not give is a finding, exhibited by the pasted command and its output.
- **Probe M** (B1, the MDE). `cd harness && uv run python -c 'from fvh import stats; print(stats.mde_two_proportions(<n>, <p0>, alpha=<alpha>, paired=<True|False>))'` with the plan's own inputs. A stated MDE that differs from the call at the stated inputs, or a call whose inputs contradict the plan's other fields (its `n` is not the number of independent units the comparison pairs, its `alpha` is not the level of the test it names), is a finding, exhibited by the pasted call.
- **Probe W** (B1 and B2, a Wilson interval). `cd harness && uv run python -c 'from fvh import stats; print(stats.wilson_interval(<k>, <n>))'` for every interval the plan or the report quotes with its counts. An interval the call does not print is a finding, exhibited by the call.
- **Probe K** (B2, and B1 where a worked $\kappa$ is quoted). Cohen's κ from a stated confusion matrix, recomputed: `python3 -c 'm=<the matrix as a list of rows, hidden verdicts down, oracle verdicts across>; n=sum(map(sum,m)); po=sum(m[i][i] for i in range(len(m)))/n; pe=sum(sum(m[i])*sum(r[i] for r in m) for i in range(len(m)))/n**2; print(round(po,3), round((po-pe)/(1-pe),3))'`. A κ the command does not print, at the matrix the answer states, is a finding.
- **Probe S** (B1, the seed lists). `python3 -c 'a=set(range(<a1>,<a2>+1)); b=set(range(<b1>,<b2>+1)); print(len(a & b))'` for each pair of seed lists the plan states. A non-zero count is a finding.
- **Probe O** (B1 and B2, the oracles' version). `sha256sum harness/q/fvh_q/oracles.py` at the commit the plan or a run record names (`git show <commit>:harness/q/fvh_q/oracles.py | sha256sum`). A recorded hash that differs is a `disclosure` finding, exhibited by the two hashes.
- **Probe R** (B2, `--report`). The report's own `Reproduce:` command, run from the course root, its output pasted; then `fvh report Q15` once it exists. A table it does not regenerate, the confusion matrix and κ included, is a `reproducibility` finding, exhibited by the diff.
- **Probe C** (B3–B5, the card pins). `sed -n '1618,1649p' …` (B3), `sed -n '1653,1677p' …` (B4) and `sed -n '1257,1282p' …` (B5) of `~/code/research/agentic-formal-veri/cheatsheets.md`, each piped to `sha256sum`, must print the hash its problem prints. Run each before grading that card.
- **Probe D** (Code-review focus, a dead step or an unneeded clause). Copy the declaration under review, with its file's `module` line and imports (for a crate file, its `use` lines) and any helper and client it calls, into `instructor/scratch/probe.dfy` or `instructor/scratch/probe.rs`; delete the one line or the one added `requires` clause in question; run `scripts/vcheck --module Q15 instructor/scratch/probe.<ext>`. Verified without it, the client included: it is not needed. Not verified: it is needed, or the proof needs it; either way, no finding.
- **Probe E** (Code-review focus C4, a prose specification). Copy your specification of a prose task (its signature, `requires` and `ensures`, with the shipped code as its body) and the given client into `instructor/scratch/probe.dfy` or `instructor/scratch/probe.rs`, add a client of your own that calls it on an input the prose covers, and run `scripts/vcheck --module Q15 instructor/scratch/probe.<ext>`. A call whose `requires` fails on a case the prose covers exhibits a specification narrower than the prose; an `ensures` the prose does not say is read, not probed (the hidden check is the learner's own run, A7).

## B1 — The pre-registration (pre-registration; dimensions: hypothesis-precision, budget-parity, variance, contamination, cheat-audit, disclosure)

The Batch plan field names the plan file and its final `plan-sha256:` binding. The harness checks that exact digest independently of the method grade. A method-only calibration fixture may describe the prospective binding; an `UNLOCKED` method grade alone cannot open a batch. After finalizing a plan, bind it and re-grade the changed pre-registration before execution. Separately executed pilot or subset plans need their own binding and gradebook row; the registered method stays fixed.

### hypothesis-precision
- **HP.a** — each claim names what is compared, the metric and its direction (one- or two-sided) or, for an estimate, the interval that would refute it: oracle separation, the feedback effect S1 − S0, and the vericoding re-judged failure rate by language. A score ≤2 quotes the claim that no stated outcome could refute.
- **HP.b** — the vericoding rate is reported on its own: a plan that compares V with S0 or S1 (a difference, a test, a ranking), quoted, scores ≤2; the arms spend different calls on a different task.
- **HP.c** — the metric has an estimator: what counts as faithful (`FAITHFUL` against the hidden reference; `UNDECIDED` counts as unfaithful), the unit (the task), the paired estimator of S1 − S0, the confusion matrix and κ for agreement, the verified-but-unfaithful rate with a Wilson interval. A score ≤2 quotes the metric with no estimator.
- **HP.d** — the 80% prediction intervals are written for each claim's estimate before any run (they sit between the markers, so Probe H covers them). Missing intervals score 2, by their quoted absence.

### budget-parity
- **BP.a** — S0 and S1 get the same number of model calls per task (four), and the plan says what one call is for each arm. Two quoted budgets for S0 and S1 that differ score ≤2.
- **BP.b** — every budget line's product recomputes (Probe B), the repeats and the sentinels included, and the totals stay within the registry's `budget`. A product that disagrees scores ≤2 with Probe B pasted.
- **BP.c** — the GPU arithmetic is per arm, states its throughput assumption and source, says that it is a lower bound for the arms whose prompts grow, and pre-registers the fallback for the case the per-arm totals exceed the ceiling. An arm-blind single figure is a gap (3); a figure that recomputes wrong from the plan's own numbers scores ≤2 with the recomputation.
- **BP.d** — the stopping rule cannot turn a rate limit or an early lead into best-of-n: no arm stops or continues on its own outcomes. A quoted rule that stops on outcomes scores ≤2.

### variance
- **VA.a** — the repeats are stated per arm; the Claude arm's repeats are "repeated runs", never "seeds". A quoted "seeds" for the Claude arm scores ≤2.
- **VA.b** — the MDE is computed with `fvh.stats.mde_two_proportions` at the stated `n`, baseline, pairing and `alpha`, and agrees with Probe M; `n` is the number of tasks, the independent unit, never task-repeats. A missing MDE scores ≤2 by its quoted absence; a value Probe M contradicts, or a call at an `n` that counts repeats as independent units, scores ≤2 with the call pasted.
- **VA.c** — every rate carries an interval: Wilson for a proportion (Probe W confirms any quoted one), a bootstrap or an equivalent stated interval for κ and for S1 − S0, resampling tasks. A rate with no interval is a gap (3); a quoted interval Probe W contradicts scores ≤2.

### contamination
- **CT.a** — each stratum's source is stated with its exposure risk: the local stratum's authorship date and the public sets flagged as possibly seen. A task set with no stated source scores ≤2.
- **CT.b** — the card contamination audit is planned on the local tasks: post-release authorship, a novelty review and perturbation probes, each with what it would show. A missing audit scores ≤2 by its quoted absence; a planned audit with no stated method is a gap (3).
- **CT.c** — the draw seeds, the local stratum's file hash and the public sets' commits and validation hashes are stated, so the draw is fixed before the data. A missing seed or commit is a gap (3) unless the plan says the draw is open, which scores ≤2 with the quoted line.

### cheat-audit
- **CA.a** — every feedback signal S1 receives is named and needs no hidden input; `implication` and `classify` run only after the batch, in the grader container. A signal that names the reference (an implication outcome, a verdict, the reference's text), quoted, scores ≤2: the hidden reference would enter the loop.
- **CA.b** — the cheat surface is named before the run: the instruments every kept specification and every V implementation passes (the learner's Q09 gate core in the verification container, zero holes, the task's code or statement frozen), the classes reported `UNKNOWN`, and `UNDECIDED` counted as unfaithful. A plan that lets a claimed success count before the gate's verdict, quoted, scores ≤2.
- **CA.c** — the exclusions say what happens to an `infra-failed` attempt, a `truncated:<cause>` attempt and a killed or refused oracle run (`unknown`, never a kill or a pass), each with its counting rule. A missing truncation rule scores ≤2 by its quoted absence.

### disclosure
- **DI.a** — the plan records what a run will record: model ids or digests, the pinned argv and settings, prompt hashes once the policy file is final, the oracles' version (Probe O), the sandbox manifest. A named item with its quoted absence is a gap (3) before the runs; it becomes a finding in the report.
- **DI.b** — the disclosure line says "a small-scale replication with model substitution" (or words to that effect), names Claude as grader and subject, and states one machine and one solver pin. Its quoted absence scores ≤2.

## The --report pass (B2)

`/grade-paper Q15 --report`, after the runs, on the report problem; the unlocked `prereg` row must exist and its hash must equal Probe H on the current file (a changed plan is a `disclosure` finding at 2).
- **RP.a** — the `Reproduce:` command regenerates every table (Probe R), the confusion matrix and κ included (Probe K on the reproduced matrix). A table it does not regenerate scores ≤2, with the diff.
- **RP.b** — every deviation from the pre-registration is listed with its reason, the oracles' version included (Probe O); a deviation the logs show and the report does not list scores ≤2, exhibited by the log line.
- **RP.c** — every claimed success carries the gate's verdict, every claimed faithful verdict is reproduced in the grader container from a clean checkout (the learner's `fvh report Q15 --judge` run, its lines pasted), and every grader-only class reads `UNKNOWN`. A claimed faithful verdict with neither, quoted, scores ≤2.
- **RP.d** — the vericoding rate is reported per language with its Wilson interval (Probe W) and on its own; a comparison of V with S0 or S1, quoted, scores ≤2.
- **RP.e** — the reward audit lists each planted specification with the oracle that catches it, and the bench audit lists each card finding; their absence is a gap (3).
- The method dimensions above apply to the report as executed; grade on method and reproducibility, never on the numbers.

### card-fidelity
- **CF.a** — Probe C prints the hash each card's problem prints. Otherwise that card is not graded ("card pin stale: the author must re-pin it").
- **CF.b** — all eight fields are filled (task shape, oracle, what is trusted, cheat surface, metric, budget, variance, artifact), each answering its own question. An empty field, or one that answers another field's question, scores 1 for that field.

- **CF.f** — wording is free: a field in your own words that agrees with the range scores 4, however short. A card's grade is the minimum over its fields; do not invent a flaw in a field that agrees.

### correctness (each card's closing line)
- **CO.a** — the closing line names one of `implication`, `kill_rate`, `vacuity` and says whether the signal needs the reference (and so whether S1 could receive it), with a reason the card's range supports. A score ≤2 quotes the range line that contradicts it. A different oracle, or the other answer on reference-freedom, with a reason the range supports, is correct: do not invent a flaw. Advisory beside the card's grade.

## Code-review focus — band: "design-judgment"

At this bar, the conventions of the earlier bars bind (the proof reads, the induction follows the recursion it unfolds, layer facts are called by name, a trigger or measure is named where it is used), plus design judgment: where each fact lives is a choice the code defends, and the proof is stable under seeds and solvers (A6).
- **C1 — a trigger chosen, not inherited (H1d, H1v).** The proof's quantified step carries an explicit trigger that no instance of the step creates, and the proof holds the terms that trigger waits for; A5 checks the form and the sweep. A5's `trigger` check sees only that a trigger is present (a tripwire): a triggered quantifier the proof does not use (Probe D: the proof still verifies with it deleted), added beside a proof that names no trigger where it needs one, is padding, the same finding as no trigger at all at grade 1, cited at `file:line`. If A5 and A6 hold and the triggered step is one the proof uses, the trigger is right: do not invent a flaw in its choice. The `BRITTLENESS` table is filled from the sweep's own output (both rows, resource counts included); a table whose numbers the sweep's raw output does not show, cited at `file:line`, is a reading note.
- **C2 — the refutation and the repair (H2d, H2v).** The refutation lemma has no parameters, verifies (A1), and its `ensures` states, at the values it chooses, every shipped `requires` clause and the negation of the shipped `ensures`; a missing clause is a finding at grade 2 only with an executable check showing that clause false at those values (Dafny: a `@Test` method with `expect`, run with `scripts/vcheck --test`), otherwise a reading note. The repair adds `requires` clauses only (A4), and the given client verifies with its check uncommented (A5). A clause Probe D shows unneeded (the lemma and the client verified without it) is a reading note on the repair's size, never below 3.
- **C3 — the one helper earns its place (H1d, H1v, H2d, H2v).** Each item's `HELPERS` area holds at most one lemma or proof fn of your own, with a statement of its own that the main proof calls. If the gate is green, a recursive proof's induction is sound: a finding here is about reading, never correctness.
- **C4 — the prose specifications say what the prose says (H3d–H5d, H3v–H5v).** Each specification is on the declaration the block names, its `requires` cover every case the prose covers, its `ensures` is the prose's conclusion, the witness's call meets every `requires`, and the shipped code is unchanged: `git diff -U0 -- dafny/Q/Q15_PracticeIIC/std/Q15_Lab2Specs.dfy verus/q/q15_practice_iic/src/lab2_specs.rs` shows only added clauses, proof lines (assertions, lemma calls, invariants, `proof { … }` blocks, spec fns of your own), uncommented client lines, witness calls and the deleted assumes. A removed or changed line of code, quoted from the diff, is a finding at grade 1, whatever the verifier says. A narrower `requires` is a finding only when Probe E exhibits the case; a stronger or different `ensures` is a finding when it says something the prose does not, quoted beside the prose. A reported verdict (A7, the specification's first run) anchors this line for that specification: `strong`, `weak`, `vacuous` or `mismatch` is a finding at grade 2, whatever Probe E shows, stated with the verdict word and the outcome words as reported (they name the side and the direction of the difference), and the specification is then read for where it differs, quoted beside the prose; never guess or state the author's specification. `undecided` anchors nothing: the reader decides it, by the prose, Probe E and the reading above. `faithful`, `context` (the learner's own definitions did not verify there: the gate's anchors rule) and `not run` leave the line to the reading above. A changed line of code stays a finding at grade 1. If the use sites, the witnesses and the gate all pass, Probe E finds no case and no reported verdict anchors a finding, the specification stands: do not invent a flaw.
- **C5 — the client speaks the protocol (H6py–H8py).** The server is started only through the given `start` or `fvh.lsp.start_server`, and stopped in every path; the reader takes exactly `Content-Length` bytes of the body and refuses what its docstring says to refuse; the end of a file's verification is decided from the server's notifications as the docstring defines it. A client the frozen tests accept is correct in shape: do not invent a flaw.
