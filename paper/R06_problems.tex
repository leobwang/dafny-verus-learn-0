\begin{problem}[proof]\label{prob:B2}
\textbf{Flagship, Cat case on paper.} Write both directions of
\[
 \mathsf{Matches}(\mathsf{Deriv}(\mathsf{Cat}(a,b),c),s)
 \Longleftrightarrow\mathsf{Matches}(\mathsf{Cat}(a,b),[c]+s).
\]
You may use the induction hypotheses on strict sub-regexes and the declared
A1b facts, but not the theorem you are proving. Introduce the semantic split
of $[c]+s$, distinguish zero from a positive split and state every shifted
slice equality and bound. Which property connects a zero split to the branch
test in the given derivative definition? Identify and instantiate the relevant
given fact. Do not assume that the left operand is nonnullable.
For the converse, cover both branches of the derivative's conditional/Alt.
State a well-founded measure that justifies every recursive invocation.
\end{problem}
\begin{problem}[proof]\label{prob:B3}
\textbf{The matcher invariant as an induction hypothesis.} A loop starts
with the original regex $r$, consumes one input character per iteration,
replaces the current regex by its derivative, and finally tests nullable.

contract, and interpret it as induction on the counter. Prove entry,
preservation and the postcondition, naming bounds, a decreasing quantity and
the required declared facts. Explain why retaining only the implication from
the original match loses an obligation at the end.
\end{problem}
