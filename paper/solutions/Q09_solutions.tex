\section*{How these are graded}
B1 and B2 are hand calculations, dimension \texttt{recomputation}: the grader
fills the rubric's statistics probe (Probes A and B) with the learner's numbers
and reads, step by step, where an error entered (the probe's \emph{carried}
column recomputes each step from the learner's own earlier values, its
\emph{fresh} column from the problem's numbers). B1(c)'s second sentence,
B1(d)'s reason and B2(b) are read under \texttt{correctness} inside the same
problem. B3 is a concept problem, dimension \texttt{correctness}: the grader
re-runs each row's command (Probe C) and tries to counterexample the row. B4 is
a paper card (\texttt{card-fidelity}, per field). The solutions below give the
answers; the rubric never states them. The pair holds no blueprint, so
\texttt{/grade-paper Q09 -{}-post} has nothing to re-score.

\section*{B1 (hand-calculation): $n = 40$ per arm, $p_0 = 0.3$}

\textbf{(a)} $p_0 q_0 = 0.3 \times 0.7 = 0.21$; $2 p_0 q_0 / n = 0.42/40 =
0.0105$; $\sqrt{0.0105} = 0.10247$; $z_a + z_b = 2.8016$; $\delta_1 = 2.8016
\times 0.10247 = 0.2871$ (28.7 points).

\textbf{(b)} At $\delta_1$: $p_1 = 0.5871$; $\bar p = (0.3 + 0.5871)/2 =
0.4435$; $2 \bar p \bar q = 2 \times 0.4435 \times 0.5565 = 0.4936$, root
$0.7026$; $p_0 q_0 + p_1 q_1 = 0.21 + 0.5871 \times 0.4129 = 0.21 + 0.2424 =
0.4524$, root $0.6726$; bracket $1.96 \times 0.7026 + 0.8416 \times 0.6726 =
1.3771 + 0.5661 = 1.9431$, squared $3.7758$; $\delta_1^2 = 0.08241$;
$n(\delta_1) = 3.7758/0.08241 = 45.8$. More than 40 tasks per arm are needed,
so $\delta_1$ is \emph{not} detectable with power $0.8$ at $n = 40$: the
first-order value is too small, because the variance at $p_1$ ($p_1 q_1 =
0.2424$) and at $\bar p$ ($0.4936 > 0.42$) is larger than at $p_0$.
\texttt{fvh.stats.mde\_two\_proportions(40, 0.3)} prints
\texttt{0.30730426184449494} (30.7 points). Substitution: at $\delta =
0.3073$, $p_1 = 0.6073$, $\bar p = 0.4537$, roots $0.7041$ and $0.6697$,
bracket $1.9436$, squared $3.7775$, $\delta^2 = 0.09444$, $n = 40.0$.

\textbf{(c)} At $\delta = 0.10$: $p_1 = 0.4$, $\bar p = 0.35$, $\sqrt{0.455} =
0.6745$, $\sqrt{0.21 + 0.24} = 0.6708$, bracket $1.3221 + 0.5646 = 1.8867$,
squared $3.5595$, $n(0.10) = 3.5595/0.01 = 356$ tasks per arm (355.9). A
40-task lab cannot detect the hoped-for 10 points: its pre-registration must
state the MDE (about 31 points) and either scope the claim to effects that
large, or change the design before the run (about 356 tasks per arm, or a
paired design and a recomputed MDE). A null result at $n = 40$ is then reported
as ``no difference larger than about 31 points'', never as ``no difference''.
Either the scoped claim or the redesign, with the MDE stated before the run, is
a full answer.

\textbf{(d)} \texttt{mde\_two\_proportions(40, 0.3, paired=True)} prints
\texttt{0.3181252951216505}. McNemar's test reads only the discordant tasks;
the formula assumes the discordance rate that independent arms would give,
$p_0 q_1 + p_1 q_0$, and arms run on the same tasks are positively correlated
and discord less, so their true MDE is smaller than this value: it errs on the
safe side. (``Larger than the unpaired one, so it is conservative'' alone names
no mechanism: a gap, 3.)

\textbf{Probe A on the pins} (the gold fixture's numbers): every step
\texttt{ok}, exit 0; its last lines print the two \texttt{fvh.stats} values above
and \texttt{n at delta1 = 45.81; n at the fvh.stats value = 40.00; n at 0.10 =
355.95}.

\textbf{Typical flaws, with their instances.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The factor 2 dropped under the root ($p_0 q_0 / n = 0.00525$, root
  $0.07246$, $\delta_1 = 0.2030$): Probe A marks \texttt{var\_term}
  \texttt{ERROR ENTERS HERE} (yours $0.00525$, carried $0.0105$); \texttt{root},
  \texttt{delta1}, \texttt{p1}, \texttt{pbar}, both roots and
  \texttt{n\_at\_delta1} ($90.3$) read \texttt{inherited}. This is the flawed
  fixture's B1; its (b) conclusion (not detectable) happens to survive.
\item $z_a$ taken one-sided ($1.645$): \texttt{z\_sum} enters ($2.4866$).
\item $\bar p$ replaced by $p_0$ in the first root ($\sqrt{0.42} = 0.6481$):
  \texttt{root\_a} enters against the learner's own $\bar p$.
\item (c) read off a ``per-arm'' formula with $n$ total ($178$): \texttt{n\_at\_10pp}
  enters.
\item (d) ``paired is conservative because it uses fewer tasks'': false of
  the design (same 40 tasks, both arms); the docstring's reason is the assumed
  discordance rate.
\end{itemize}

\section*{B2 (hand-calculation): a six-row sweep}

\textbf{(a)} Mean $(38\,400 + 45\,900 + 66\,300)/3 = 50\,200$; deviations
$-11\,800$, $-4\,300$, $+16\,100$; squares $139\,240\,000$, $18\,490\,000$,
$259\,210\,000$, sum $416\,940\,000$; population variance $416\,940\,000/3 =
138\,980\,000$; standard deviation $11\,789$; CV $11\,789/50\,200 = 0.2348$
(23.5\,\%): above 20\,\%, so \texttt{scripts/vcheck} flags it (advisory, never
gating).

\textbf{(b)} Not stable: the row \emph{4.14.1 seed 3} is out of resource, and
stable means verified in every row (seeds 1--3 under both bundled solvers).
The CV adds nothing to that decision: the flag is advisory, a cost signal (here
a hint that the proof is near the edge, which the failing row confirms), and a
verified-everywhere proof with a flagged CV is still stable. Counts under two
solvers are counts of two different programs (different search heuristics), so
pooling them would measure the solver difference, not seed noise:
\texttt{scripts/vcheck} computes each spread over one solver's seeds.

\textbf{(c)} \texttt{fvh.stats.wilson\_interval(17, 22)} prints
\texttt{(0.5656004682477829, 0.898769600147515)}. A claim: ``17 of my 22
filled Dafny proofs of Q04--Q09 are stable (both bundled solvers, seeds 1--3),
77\,\% (95\,\% Wilson interval 57--90\,\%); one machine, one solver pair,
three seeds; I wrote the proofs I measured.'' The interval treats the 22 proofs
as a sample of the proofs this learner writes; it is not a property of the
verifier.

\textbf{Probe B on the pins:} every step \texttt{ok} with the gold fixture's
numbers; the probe prints \texttt{deviations from the mean: [-11800.0,
-4300.0, 16100.0]}, \texttt{CV 0.2348: above the 20\% flag} and the Wilson
interval above.

\textbf{Typical flaws.} The sample divisor ($n - 1 = 2$: variance
$208\,470\,000$, CV $0.2876$): Probe B marks \texttt{variance} \texttt{ERROR ENTERS
HERE}; the problem fixes the population divisor, as the tool computes it. All
six counts pooled (CV over six rows): the problem asks for 4.12.1's three. (b)
``stable, because the CV is below 30\,\%'' or ``unstable, because the CV is
above 20\,\%'': the verdicts decide, the CV never does. B2 is the flawed
fixture's clean control.
