\begin{problem}[concept]\label{prob:B1}
\textbf{Which contracts may stand in for the trait's} (5 minutes). A trait
declares one method, \dfy{Next(x: int) returns (y: int)} (Verus: a method
\vrs{next} with result \vrs{y}), with the contract
\[ P_T(x) \;\equiv\; 0 \le x \le 10 \quad\text{(requires)}, \qquad
   Q_T(x, y) \;\equiv\; y > x \quad\text{(ensures)}. \]
A generic client is proved once, from $P_T$ and $Q_T$ alone, for every
implementation. An implementation whose method carries a contract
$(P_I, Q_I)$ of its own may stand in for the trait's exactly when both
conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $P_T(x) \Rightarrow P_I(x)$ for every $x$;
\item[(ii)] $P_T(x) \wedge Q_I(x, y) \Rightarrow Q_T(x, y)$ for every $x$ and
  $y$.
\end{enumerate}
Five candidates $(P_I, Q_I)$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[$C_1$] $x \ge 0$; \quad $y > x$
\item[$C_2$] $1 \le x \le 10$; \quad $y > x$
\item[$C_3$] $0 \le x \le 10$; \quad $y = x + 1$
\item[$C_4$] \textsf{true}; \quad $y \ge x$
\item[$C_5$] $x \le 10$; \quad $x \ge 0 \Rightarrow y > x$
\end{enumerate}
For each: \textbf{(a)} does (i) hold? does (ii) hold? \textbf{(b)} for every
condition that fails, a witness: for (i), an $x$ at which $P_T$ holds and $P_I$
does not; for (ii), a pair $(x, y)$ at which $P_T$ and $Q_I$ hold and $Q_T$
does not. \textbf{(c)} In one line: which of the two conditions protects the
generic client's call, and which protects what the client concludes after the
call?
\end{problem}
