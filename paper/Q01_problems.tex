\begin{problem}[paper-card]\label{prob:B1}
\textbf{Paper card 02.3, A benchmark for vericoding: formally verified program
synthesis} (15 minutes). Fill the card on its eight fields. Each field is
graded against the pinned range of your own \texttt{cheatsheets.md}: lines
642--668, the entry from its heading to its \emph{Why it matters} paragraph, in
the copy the command below names. The pin is the sha256 of exactly those
lines, computed with the first line below; it must print the second.
{\small
\begin{verbatim}
sed -n '642,668p' ~/code/research/agentic-formal-veri/cheatsheets.md | sha256sum
0cb3f94d3982d6d1826d4ef44b71a1a7b634d27d249086bc2281ef61febe5a38  -
\end{verbatim}
}
What each field asks:
\begin{description}
\item[Task shape:] what goes in, what must come out, in which languages, and
  how large a task is.
\item[Oracle:] what decides that a run succeeded.
\item[Trust:] what the success criterion takes on faith.
\item[Cheat surface:] how a run could be counted as a success without being
  one.
\item[Metric:] the number reported, and what it is computed over.
\item[Budget:] what each task gets: models, attempts or turns, tokens, time.
\item[Variance:] how the spread of the result is measured (repeated runs,
  intervals), or that it is not.
\item[Artifact:] what was released for others to reuse.
\end{description}
Write each field in your own words, a sentence or two. Where the range says
nothing about a field, write that, and what you would need to know. A hash
that no longer matches means the card file changed: the grader refuses the
card until the pin is renewed.
\end{problem}
